# The fleet list

Status: first draft, 6 November 2026, by the lore chat for Jason's review. Every shipbuilder in known space and every hull it makes, with the ones already in the catalogue marked. Names and roles only; sizes, mounts, stats and build order are the lead's. Classes are the two ladders in `lore.md`, "Hull classes". The handover for the lead starts at `lore-handover.md`; the per-hull table is `hulls.md` (generated from the registry), the definitions are `hull-terms.md`, the T3 abilities are `t3-capabilities.md`.

Status column: **built** (in the catalogue today), **named** (a working hull exists and this is its name), **draft** (nothing exists; a name and a role for when it does).

## The shape of it

Sixteen companies build hulls. Four are naval yards and build only for their navy. Two are **independents**, companies with no flag that build civil and combat hulls alike and sell to anyone with a Registry licence (see "Independents"). The rest build civilian hulls, and three of them overlap on couriers, haulers and freighters so that NPC traffic shows different hulls doing the same job.

## Union Defence Works and the Union pattern

Decided 7 November 2026. The Belt has no naval yard. **The Union Militia is a rota, not a fleet**: it owns no hulls, and cooperatives and clans contribute ships and crews on shifts. **Union Defence Works is the refit shop**: it takes captured, bought-cheap or copied hulls and rebuilds them to a Militia standard, the **Union pattern**. "Wildcat" is a standard applied to a hull, not a hull from a slipway; two Wildcats can have started life as a Kessari Notice and a Citation, and the source shows under the Belt shapes. Strike words for the standards.

- **No T1 or T2 in the Belt.** A Belt hull's generation is its source's, and the Belt never retires anything. The Belt's rows are Union pattern (numbers) and clan (quality).
- **No civilian grade, because the tag is a state.** Every Belt hull is civilian. On Militia shift it carries the **Militia tag** (the Belt's Navy tag: better stats, hazard stripes and the Union mark painted on); off shift it goes home in cooperative colours with the tag off. The licence is the rota: standing with a cooperative, duty is shifts. Mechanically the same grade flag the other navies use, toggled by rota rather than purchase.
- **Sold only in the Belt**, for cooperative standing and cheap credits. Every one a little different. Rebuilt captures with old mounts: worse than a production hull, cheaper and rougher than a retired navy one. The pirate's favourite source; the Black Strike's Writs are Union-pattern rebuilds in all but paperwork.
- **Who answers a beacon in the Belt** depends on which cooperative holds the station and which clan is nearby: the same Wildcat in three cooperatives' colours, and sometimes a Vane out of nowhere.

## Subcraft and drones

Decided 6 November 2026. A **subcraft** is any crewed small craft carried in a bay, whatever its job: combat, mining, salvage, repair, rescue. In effect a subcraft is a very heavy drone with a crew and better capabilities. Subcraft are flown by NPC crews, never by the player. A **fighter** is a combat subcraft. A **drone** is the uncrewed kind. Three rules:

1. **Subcraft and drones are products, not hulls.** They live in bays, are bought like modules, and companies build them. Navy yards build the carriers; what the carriers carry comes from specialist makers. Mars is the exception by character: Arcadia Works builds the Navy's fighters because the state builds everything.
2. **Military variants come from the same makers.** A maker's civilian line is open market. Its combat line is sold like navy hulls: light combat fighters and light combat drones openly, heavy ones behind standing or a navy licence.
3. **Industrial subcraft and drones never go military.** A mining or salvage subcraft has mining or salvage mounts and no weapon mounts. It is not a refit of a fighter and cannot become one.

Capital-class industrial ships (the Headframe, the Winder, the Smelter, the Breaker) are carriers in all but name: their bays launch mining and salvage subcraft and drones the way a Hellas launches fighters.

## Navy grade, civilian grade and retired navy hulls

Decided 7 November 2026. **Navy** is a tag on a hull, not a generation. A Navy-tagged hull has better stats than the same hull without the tag (how much better is the lead's call). The tag is what a licence buys; it is not a different ship.

- **Civilian-grade navy hulls.** Each naval yard sells its current corvette and patrol boat in two versions: Navy (behind the licence, with duties) and civilian (the same hull, the same mark, no Navy tag, open market, company livery). Frigate and above are built Navy only. A civilian-grade hull can be brought up to Navy grade later with the licence, so a pilot who starts in a civilian Warrant and joins the Charter Fleet keeps the ship. Mechanically: the same hull id with a grade flag, not a second hull.
- **Retired navy hulls (surplus).** When a navy retires a mark, the old hulls go to the open market whole: Navy tag intact, mounts intact, no licence needed. They are still powerful and still expensive; what they lack is the current mark's refinements and the yard's support. This replaces the earlier idea of demilitarised surplus. The navy livery is painted over because the ship is out of service, and the lines show through.
- **Legacy** stays what it was: an old civilian mark. Legacy is cheap; retired navy is not.
- **Fighters follow the same rules.** The light fighter is the corvette of the bays: Navy or civilian grade (Vantage Foil, Arcadia Fighter Mk V). The heavy fighter is Navy only (Vantage Sabre, Arcadia Heavy Fighter Mk II). Each has a retired mark sold whole with the Navy tag: Vantage Dirk and Cutlass, Arcadia Fighter Mk IV and Heavy Fighter Mk I. Vantage names its craft for blades.

| Navy | Civilian grade | Retired marks on the market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earth, the Charter Fleet | Citation and Warrant, sold openly | Kessari Notice, Writ, Decree and Ruling, sold by contract: the backbone of the Marches |
| Mars, the Martian Navy | Deimos and Phobos, sold to Martian citizens only | Deimos Mk I and Phobos Mk II, released once the replacement mark is in service, Martian buyers first; the Navy still wants stolen current hulls back |
| Venus, the Coastguard | Sedna and Lakshmi, sold openly | Sedna '218 and Lakshmi '205, sold with the Coastguard's guns removed but the Navy tag intact; fit your own |
| The Belt, the Union Militia | No grades: the Militia tag is a rota state on any Belt hull (see "Union Defence Works and the Union pattern") | None; the Militia never retires anything |

Retired navy hulls go up to destroyer (the Ruling) where a navy retires that big; nobody sells a retired cruiser. The Mars exception above resolves an earlier contradiction: Mars does sell, but only its own retired marks and its own citizens first.

For the lead: a `navy` tag on hull entries; a `grade` on owned ships (civilian or navy) for the four corvette and patrol hulls; a retired navy hull is its own hull entry (its own mounts and stats) pointing at the mark that replaced it, Navy tag set, open-market price.

On the registry: a civilian-grade hull is a livery toggle on the production hull's page, not its own entry, because it is the same design on the same date. Retired navy hulls and legacy marks are their own entries, because they are different designs.

## Special operations: the T3 line

Decided 7 November 2026. Special operations vessels are a navy's advanced offensive capability: a hull built around exactly one capability, the best in the game at that one job, and below a production hull at something else. Never a generalist, never a pattern of an existing hull, never on the open market. Stealth is the line's theme rather than a hull of its own: dark unregistered liveries, cold running, off the board when dark.

### The hunting doctrine

Three ships, one job: find a target, land on it, hold it, kill it or hold it until the fleet arrives.

| Role | Class | Capability | What it costs | The tell on the hull |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Watcher** | Frigate (corvette for the clans) | Fast and agile despite its size, cold-running, thin as paper. Finds the target, shadows it off the board, holds a lock. Its lock is a jump target. | Does no real damage; dies to anything that catches it | More engine and sensor than hull; exposed structure; no plate |
| **Hunter** | Cruiser | The brick. Jumps onto a lock, lands at close range and pins: cuts the target's burn, slows its turn, drags its capacitor. Survives long enough for the fleet to arrive, and is why the fleet knows where to arrive. | Slow, modest guns, long jump cooldown, no shields for a few seconds after landing | Heavy plate round a drive ring that is not human; projector arrays where the guns should be |
| **Striker** | Battleship (frigate for the clans) | Stripped down and bristling. Jumps in behind the hunter and dumps alpha far above its class. Flown in packs by elite crews. | Low defences; with the cooldown it is committed once it lands: it kills or it dies there | A hull built around its weapons, little armour, the drive ring |

### Jumping

- A **jump** is a short in-system hop without a gate, from a standing start to a point at close range. It needs a **jump target**: a watcher's lock on a ship, a friendly fleet beacon, or a station beacon. No target, no jump. Jumps never cross systems; the gates keep that.
- The drive is **gate-born**: built round salvaged Lattice material from the Shoals and the Dimming, barely understood. Single digits exist per power. It is banned under the Ceres Accord's Open Lanes clause, so every navy denies having one, and the Accord's lawyers hate the hunter's pin as much as the drive.
- Arriving is loud: a flash on the board, a capacitor dump, a few seconds without shields. A long cooldown before the next jump, so a jump is a commitment.
- The clans have no drives unless stolen. A clan hunting pack burns in behind its watcher.

### How navy NPCs use it

A hunting group is what a power sends after a wanted ship, a dark ship that will not stop, or a pirate it has decided to finish. The watcher arrives first and may never be seen. The hunter lands on the target. The strikers land behind it. The regular patrol arrives last, by burn, to clean up. A lone hunter is a story beat (it grabbed you and called for backup); a full group is the game saying you have gone too far. A pilot meets the doctrine from both ends: as prey when their standing goes bad, and as crew at the top of a navy's standing ladder.

## Art direction

Decided 6 November 2026, for whoever draws hulls (the registry page, the canvas, the client's ship art).

- **No basic shapes, and no triangles.** A hull is never a wedge, a dart or a primitive with a nose on it. Silhouettes are built from several volumes: a spine, a hull, pods, trusses, tanks, a bridge. Earth's shape language is layered panels and chamfered blocks, not wedges.
- **Good detail.** Panel lines, hatches, docking collars, running lights, antennae, radiators, drive bells, and the company's signature features: Tessaract's container racks, Belt Forge's exposed trusses and clamps, Seraphine's overlapping plates, Arcadia's identical bolted modules, Solace's crosses and floodlights. Detail is for the close-up and the fitting window; the silhouette must still read at game zoom, which is what the registry's full, no-detail and silhouette levels are for.
- **Colour tells you three things at once:** the role (industrial hazard markings, medical white, freight company liveries, navy grey or the navy's colour), the company (its primary), and the power (the registry flash). A pilot should be able to say "Belt industrial", "Earth navy" or "Venusian medical" from colour alone before reading a name.
- **Mounts show.** Every fitted module has a visible silhouette at its mount, as today; a hull with spare mounts shows the empty sockets up close.

## Generations: T1, T2 and T3

Jason's direction (6 November): older hulls that are out of production fill a used market, cheaper, and are the way in for new pilots; the generations are where the real variety comes from. Jason's tiers (7 November): T1 is the older marks, T2 the current ones and their patterns, T3 special operations and the clans. The tier is the price band and the rarity; the generation is what the hull is:

| Tier | Generation | What it is | Where you get it | Price and quality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T1** | Legacy | An earlier civilian mark, no longer built. Companies stopped making it; crews never stopped flying it. | The used market at any ship mart, pirate fences, salvage | Cheap, fewer slots or weaker stats, every one a little different (wear, repairs, kitbash) |
| **T1** | Retired navy (surplus) | A navy mark the navy has replaced, sold whole with the Navy tag intact | The open market, where that navy releases them (see "Navy grade, civilian grade and retired navy hulls") | Expensive and strong; older mounts and no yard support; the livery painted over |
