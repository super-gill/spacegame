# Coordination

How the Spacegame chats work together. Every chat working on the repo reads this file first and updates it last.

**The repo is the memory.** No chat needs to remember the whole project: the design docs, `docs/changelog.md`, `docs/roadmap.md`, this file and the git history hold it. A chat that has lost track rereads them; a chat that is getting heavy hands over to a fresh one.

## Who does what

| Who | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Jason** | The designer. Decides what the game is, sets priorities, plays the builds. Does not code. |
| **Lead** (the current lead chat; "Lead 5" since 6 November 2026) | All code: sim, client, tests, docs, versions, releases, `main`. Puts design questions to Jason and keeps this file current. |
| **Lore** (its own long-lived chat, branch `ws/lore`) | Canon: `docs/lore.md` and `docs/races.md`, names for everything, in conversation with Jason. Never code, catalogue data or numbers. The lead merges its branch and applies its names. |
| **Focused passes** (occasional) | A chat opened for one job with a clear brief, such as an interface restyle, and closed when it is merged. The lead writes the brief here and the opener for Jason when one is wanted. |

Chats cannot message each other: this file, branches and commits are the only channel, and Jason carries anything urgent.

## Session routine

**Lead, start:** `git fetch`, pull `main`, read this file and the handover doc in the project, check "Lore asks" and "Questions for Jason".

**Lead, end:** a log entry (date, what changed, what is next), changelog lines under "Unreleased", the roadmap if a milestone moved, and Jason's commit message.

**Lore, start and end:** rebase `ws/lore` on `main`, work through "Lore asks" with Jason, record answers in `docs/lore.md`, add a log entry in the branch, push, and tell Jason it is ready for the lead to merge.

**Handing over to a new lead:** when a lead chat gets heavy, it updates the handover doc in the project (state, next task, practical notes) and this file, pushes, and gives Jason a one-line opener for the new chat. Nothing else carries over.

**Rules for every chat:** only the lead pushes to `main` and bumps versions; `npm test` passes before every push; design questions go to Jason rather than being guessed and built on.

## Questions for Jason

Open design questions. Answers go to "Decisions".

- **How many small ships should beat a stock frigate?** (2026-11-06). The crowd clearer is settled (Decisions). Still open: the militia (stock) frigate beats four scripted Warrant brawlers in the lab; should a swarm of a given size beat it? Lead's suggestion: first make swarms smarter (spread round the target, gather at the edge of swivel range, commit together; kiters hold at gun range), which serves the arena, pirate packs and law ships alike, then tune frigate shields and plate to Jason's feel in the arena.
- **Law (7): distress calls and escalation** (2026-11-05, Jason; for the law design). Law enforcement spawns in rather than only patrolling: an attacked ship in high security space can make a distress call, and the response depends on its standing in that system. Good standing and not a known criminal: the local force sends a small ship or two to look, engages hostiles alongside you, and escalates if it needs to. The local force can call the navy as the next step, bringing heavy naval ships. The aim: law is very hard to beat, not impossible. Lead's notes: this fits the decided escalating local law (watch, then its reserve, then the holder's navy, thinner farther out); the distress call is a pilot command with a cooldown, answered by a spawner keyed to the system's security band and the caller's standing, with arrival times so a quick kill can still escape; to settle when law is designed: whether NPC victims call too, what "known criminal" means (ties to who is to blame, below), and whether the navy leaves once the fight is over.
- **Law (7): who is to blame** (2026-11-05, to discuss when law is designed). Jason's first thought: shooting is the illegal part, so whoever shoots first is the criminal; theft counts too. Lead's note: EVE's model fits that shape: the first unprovoked attack or a theft flags the pilot, and the victim (and anyone defending them) may fire back legally. Lore already says crime must be witnessed. To settle: what counts as theft (loot from someone's wreck, someone's cache, an empty ship), how long a flag lasts, whether helping a flagged pilot spreads the flag, and what witnesses are.

## Contracts

Interfaces the client, saves and any focused-pass chat rely on. Owned by the lead.

Changing any of these needs the lead. All live in `packages/sim/src` and are exported from `index.ts`.

- **Catalogue types** (`types.ts`): `HullSpec`, `MountPoint`, `ModuleSpec`, `Loadout`, `HullBonus`, `Manufacturer`. Hulls, modules and loadouts are data; adding entries is fine, changing the shape is a contract change.
- **Derived stats** (`stats.ts`): `ShipStats`, `shipStats(world, ship)`, `fitStats(cat, hull, fit, cargoMass)`. Nothing stores performance; everything calls these.
- **Fitting** (`fitting.ts`): `FitRefusal`, `mountRefusal`, `planRefit`, `fitModule`, `unfitModule`. The client never applies fitting rules itself; it asks `planRefit`.
- **Ship fit** (`types.ts`): `Ship.fit` maps mount id to module id; `WeaponState` is keyed by `mountId`.
- **Pilot storage** (`types.ts`): `Pilot.storage` maps station id to module id to count.
- **Save format** (`save.ts`): `SAVE_VERSION`. Only the lead bumps it.
- **Sensor range** (`types.ts`, `stats.ts`): `ShipStats.sensorRange`, units, from the fitted radar only (6c part 2; `HullSpec.sensorRange` is gone). Zero with no radar: show that pilot no ships at all. A pilot is shown only the ships within their ship's sensor range; stations and bodies are always shown.

## Ideas inbox

Jason's ideas that are not scheduled yet. The lead triages them into the roadmap or "not now".

- **Station functions on ships** (2026-11-03, later): bigger ships could carry station services such as outfitting; player stations, or station ships, may follow.
- **Multiboxing is a built-in feature** (2026-11-05, Jason; may land after the server split, but the split must not block it): one person can run several clients at once, each a fully fledged character, as in EVE: two playstreams side by side, working together or doing separate things. In game each character is an entirely independent person; nothing links them. Lead's notes: today the pilot id lives in the browser's localStorage (`spacegame.pilot`), so two tabs in one browser are the same pilot and also fight over the local save; the split must replace that with choosing a character on connect (a person or account holds several pilots, one connection per pilot at a time). Characters stay fully separate in the sim (own credits, ships, storage, standing), so nothing in the sim changes: pilots are already independent. Answered (Jason): characters are independent people in game, so they trade and fight like any two pilots.
- **Connection meter** (2026-11-05, with the server split): a small, dismissable latency readout tucked in a corner, coloured by round-trip time (for example green under 60 ms, amber to 150, red above), showing the number on hover. Comes with the first networked build so relayed Tailscale connections are easy to spot. Lead's note: the server echoes a ping message every second or two; the client keeps a short rolling average so the colour does not flicker; dismissed state is a client setting.
- **More ships per size** (2026-11-05): several ships at each size, with slight differences, better and worse in different areas, rather than one ship per rung of the ladder. A content pass once the mining ladder has settled.
- **Industrial command ship** (2026-11-05, later): an industrial capital that coordinates NPC miners, carries mining fighters and a huge hold. Needs hired NPC agents first.
- **Boarding abandoned ships** (2026-11-03): taking a ship nobody owns, or someone else's. Belongs with law (7) and salvage (9). Owning ships, swapping in space and pods are scheduled in 6b.
- **Capacitor battery module** (2026-11-04, later, fitting polish): a fitted battery that adds capacitor, so a small ship can carry a big pool at a mass and price cost. Jason: the capacitor is a hull property for now; a battery feels like a later tuning and polishing decision.
- **Missiles, torpedoes and CIWS** (2026-11-04, scheduled: 6e on the roadmap, designed in `docs/weapons.md`): missiles fast and cheap with modest damage; torpedoes heavy and slow with massive damage. The turret's slow, automatic tracking suits close-in weapon systems: turrets may simply become CIWS, engaging the closest hostile missile or torpedo (or ship) on their own, or a locked target if one is assigned. It has to be automatic, because a pilot cannot click a missile reliably while flying and fighting. Lead's notes: missiles and torpedoes are projectiles with a lock and their own steering (the same flight rules as ships); they need a signature so a CIWS can lock them, and ammunition (6e already plans ammunition for projectile weapons and missiles); assigning a turret to a lock (`WeaponState.lockTarget`, already in place) or to point defence is the 6e "systems assignable to locks" work. Fixed guns keep out-damaging turrets of the same size. **Countermeasures** (Jason, same day): chaff, flares and decoys at the top level, as countermeasure modules that each carry a mix from different manufacturers (brands trading what they carry, how many, mass and price). Lead's note: each counters a seeker type, so missiles and torpedoes need a guidance kind (radar, heat, signature) for the match to mean something; launching is a press, so it is a fitted system with charges, like ammunition.
- **Bastion mode** (2026-11-04, later: with the first line ship or capital): really big ships can shut their propulsion down and sit still behind a full, all-round shield. Pairs with the shield-arc lore (the drive's wash is why the stern is open). Lead's notes: a hull capability (`HullSpec.bastion`), an input to enter and leave it with a spin-up time, no thrust while in it; NPC capitals use it when outnumbered.
- **Fuel** (2026-11-04, waiting: Jason, "fuel can wait for now"; with the map (8) at the latest): ships need fuel, stored in tanks, and tanks are modules (mass, a mount, capacity; bigger tanks for explorers). One fuel for everything (thrust now, faster-than-light later), so range is one number a pilot can plan exploration around.
